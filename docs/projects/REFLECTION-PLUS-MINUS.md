## 01 · Proposition

REFLECTION± investigates coordinated optical input, computation and optical output as a reciprocal system:

**LIGHT IN → COMPUTE → LIGHT OUT → ENVIRONMENT / HUMAN RESPONSE → LIGHT IN**

The research distinguishes passive optical behaviour, optical acquisition, digital computation, optical emission/modulation and human/environment feedback. A passive mirror does not compute; a camera is not a mirror; a display is not a reflective surface.

## 02 · Research questions

- How should physical optics, acquisition, computation and output be co-designed and measured?
- Which camera, event-sensing, display and timing interfaces are appropriate to particular tasks?
- How can end-to-end latency be measured as a distribution under stated workloads?
- How can the physical reflection remain meaningful while digital information is introduced?
- How can consent, local processing, retention and participant control be made explicit?

## 03 · Prior art and existing systems

The canonical IKAI report surveys computational optical imaging, event-based vision, MIPI CSI-2 and DSI-2, Linux V4L2 and DRM/KMS, IEEE 1588 PTP, display technologies and reflective/augmented-reality mirror research. These are research leads and candidate interfaces, not a selected or integrated stack.

## 04 · Architecture

The research model separates:
1. Optical scene and illumination.
2. Acquisition and sensing.
3. Device transport.
4. Time and synchronisation.
5. Compute and orchestration.
6. Rendering and output transport.
7. Optical output.
8. Human/environment interaction.
9. Governance, consent and safety.

The initial strategy is electronic-compute-first, native/local-first, standards-aware and measurement-led. Photonic computing remains a comparative research track, not a prerequisite.

## 05 · Experiments

**Proposed, not completed:** establish a stationary mirror/display demonstrator; measure optical usability, display contrast, geometric registration, timing and end-to-end latency; compare frame-based and event-based sensing only against task-specific criteria; verify offline operation and participant-controlled data handling.

No experiment completion or result is claimed by this publication record.

## 06 · Findings and limitations

The source research identifies important distinctions between physical reflection and camera/display simulation, and between shared clock synchronisation and true end-to-end timing. A single FPS figure or average latency is insufficient evidence.

**Non-claims:** no integrated REFLECTION± system, working mirror, validated sensor pipeline, deployment, procurement decision or product is claimed. IKAI remains RESEARCH / WIP.
