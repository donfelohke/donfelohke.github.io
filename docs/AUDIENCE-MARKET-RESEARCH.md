# Audience, Clientele and Data-Informed Webcopy Research

**Repository:** `donfelohke/donfelohke.github.io`  
**Research date:** 2026-10-25  
**Status:** Research and copy recommendations; not evidence of conversion performance.

## Executive recommendation

Make the homepage useful to three distinct audiences, in this order:

1. **Local loc-care customers** — the clearest consumer offer and shortest path to a WhatsApp enquiry.
2. **Small organisations and individuals needing practical IT support** — a problem-led technical enquiry path under NAKEBUSEN DIGITAL / WARENA.
3. **Research, cultural and creative collaborators** — FELOH, GENJE and IKAI as the deeper practice and portfolio layer.

The homepage should not force all audiences through one generic brand story. Keep a shared identity and philosophy, but give each audience its own offer, proof requirements and call to action. Maintain the approved text-only webpage policy: no inline graphics, image embeds, graphic favicon, or social-preview image. Link to the separate Gallery repository only as a text destination.

## Evidence reviewed

### 1. Local context: Eldoret

The City of Eldoret's Urban Integrated Development Plan reports a 2019 resident population of 475,716 and presents later population figures as projections, not a fresh census count. It describes Eldoret as a regional hub and transit city with in-migration and demand for urban services. Source: https://eldoretcity.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CoE-IDeP-2026.pdf

The Uasin Gishu County CIDP 2023–2027 also identifies urbanisation and growth in Eldoret and other centres. Source: https://uasingishu.go.ke/index.php/download/county-integrated-development-plan-cidp-2023-2027

**What this supports:** Eldoret is a meaningful local service market to prioritise and a reasonable geographic phrase for local search copy.

**What it does not prove:** demand for loc maintenance, the number of loc-wearing residents, willingness to pay at the listed prices, competitor share, or NAKEBUSEN's likely conversion rate. Those require direct customer and competitor research.

### 2. Digital access and mobile-first design

The Communications Authority of Kenya reported that by June 2025, smartphone penetration was 83.5%, data subscriptions totalled 58.5 million, and mobile broadband accounted for 78.2% of data subscriptions. These are national communications indicators; subscriptions and penetration rates are not equivalent to unique people or a local customer count. Source: https://www.ca.go.ke/mobile-internet-and-tech-services-surge-kenya-digital-shift-accelerates

The KNBS 2026 Economic Survey also reports continued growth in Kenya's ICT sector and 53.8 million fixed and wireless broadband subscriptions. Source: https://www.knbs.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026-Economic-Survey-Popular-version.pdf

**What this supports:** a lightweight, mobile-first page, readable pricing, direct WhatsApp/telephone links, and minimal loading dependencies are sensible design choices.

**What it does not prove:** that any specific visitor prefers WhatsApp, that this page receives a particular amount of mobile traffic, or that a given CTA converts. Instrument and measure only if privacy-respecting analytics are later approved; do not add trackers by default.

## Audience segments and messaging

### A. Loc-care customers — primary conversion path

**Likely jobs to be done (hypotheses to validate):**
- Maintain neat, comfortable locs.
- Understand the starting price before contacting the loctician.
- Find a service provider in or near Eldoret Town.
- Discuss a particular loc style, maintenance need, materials and availability.

**Copy principles:** use everyday language, show service names and starting prices early, state the location, and make the next step explicit. Avoid gender assumptions, unsupported claims about hair health, invented testimonials, guarantees, or claims that every style is suitable for every client.

**Recommended draft:**

> **NAKEBUSEN LOCS — Your Crown. Your Craft.**  
> Loc care in Eldoret Town / Karibu Mdosi. Retouching, styling, Sisterlocs retightening and artificial loc installation, with the work and price discussed clearly before you commit.
>
> **Starting prices**  
> Retouch & Styling — KES 700  
> Sisterlocs Retie — KES 1,500  
> Artificial Locs Installation — KES 3,500
>
> Send a WhatsApp enquiry to discuss your locs, preferred style, availability and final quote. An enquiry is not a confirmed booking.

**CTA:** “Request a Loc-Care Booking” (link to WhatsApp with a loc-care-specific prefilled message).

**Proof to collect before adding:** permissioned portfolio items in the separate Gallery repository, consented client feedback, dated service examples, and an accurate description of appointment arrangements. Never publish client images or quotes without permission.

### B. Practical technology clients — qualified enquiry path

**Potential audiences (hypotheses, not proven customer segments):** small businesses, offices, schools, laboratories, cybercafés, community organisations and individuals who need reliable computer or network support.

**Likely jobs to be done:** get a computer working, improve local Wi-Fi/LAN, configure routers, maintain a server or lab, or reduce dependence on systems that fail when internet connectivity is unavailable.

**Recommended draft:**

> **NAKEBUSEN DIGITAL — Powered by WARENA**  
> Practical GNU/Linux, computer and network support for people and organisations that need systems they can understand and maintain.
>
> From computer troubleshooting and local networks to router/server setup and offline-capable environments, each engagement starts with your actual equipment, constraints and intended result. Scope, feasibility, pricing and support are agreed before work begins.

**CTA:** “Discuss a Technical Project” (technical-specific WhatsApp message).

**Proof to collect before adding:** redacted case studies, documented before/after technical outcomes, supported hardware/software scope, response/service boundaries, and examples of reproducible or maintainable setups. Do not imply a formal SLA, certification, warranty, or enterprise capacity unless documented.

### C. Research and creative collaborators — credibility path

**Potential audiences:** researchers, artists, engineers, live coders, movement practitioners and institutions interested in culture, computation and human-centred tools.

**Recommended draft:**

> **FELOH / GENJE / IKAI — Culture, Computation and Practice**  
> A developing body of work connecting creative technology, movement, living memory and reflective computing. Explore the research, working notes and public artefacts, or get in touch to propose a collaboration.
>
> IKAI is a work in progress: a research initiative, not a released commercial product.

**CTA:** “Explore the Work” / “Discuss a Collaboration”.

**Proof to publish:** linked repositories, dated research notes, reproducible artefacts, clearly labelled work-in-progress status, and precise attribution. Distinguish concepts, experiments, prototypes and released implementations.

## Search and metadata recommendations

- Keep one accurate page title and one concise meta description that name the primary service/brand and Kenya; avoid trying to rank equally for unrelated services in one keyword-stuffed sentence.
- Keep Eldoret as the primary local phrase for current loc-care acquisition. Mention Lodwar/Turkana as background only where current service availability is clear.
- Use descriptive visible link text such as “NAKEBUSEN Gallery” rather than “click here”.
- Keep Organization structured data factual. Do not mark unverified service locations, opening hours, reviews, price ranges or booking capabilities as confirmed.
- Align Open Graph/Twitter text with the text-only policy; do not add image metadata if that conflicts with the approved no-graphics rule.
- Keep the page fast, semantic, keyboard-accessible, mobile-first, and usable without external fonts, scripts or image requests.

## Research gaps and next validation steps

The available public data gives broad geographic and digital context, not customer-level demand evidence. Do not describe the following as known until researched:

- local competitor names, service menus, prices and availability;
- search demand or search-volume estimates for loc services in Eldoret;
- current customer demographics, most-requested styles and price sensitivity;
- enquiry-to-booking conversion, cancellation rate, repeat visits and customer acquisition cost;
- which technical service generates the most qualified enquiries;
- website visits, referral sources, mobile/desktop split and CTA performance.

**Low-cost validation plan:**
1. Keep a simple enquiry log with date, service requested, source/referral, whether a booking was confirmed, and outcome; collect no unnecessary personal data.
2. Ask willing customers which service wording, location phrase and booking information helped them decide.
3. Record competitor evidence from publicly visible menus and listings with URL and date; do not infer quality from price alone.
4. Review enquiry patterns monthly before changing prices or expanding the catalogue.
5. Use page-specific prefilled WhatsApp messages to distinguish loc-care from technical enquiries without adding third-party tracking.
6. Revisit copy only when evidence changes; preserve previous wording and the reason for each change in Git history.
