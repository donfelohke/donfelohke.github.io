# NAKEBUSEN Market & Customer Research

**Research date:** 26 October 2026  
**Scope:** NAKEBUSEN CROWN (loc care), NAKEBUSEN DIGITAL / WARENA (technical services), and @donfelohke (creative practice)  
**Status:** Research synthesis and validation plan — not a verified market-size study or financial forecast.

## Executive assessment

Current strategic hypotheses:

1. **CROWN:** the clearest near-term offer to validate because services and starting prices already exist. Improve booking clarity, service scope, repeat visits and margin tracking.
2. **DIGITAL / WARENA:** validate a narrow technical offer against actual buyer problems before competing as a generic IT provider.
3. **@donfelohke:** document and publish actual creative work, then test specific performance, collaboration and workshop opportunities before investing in products or events.

These priorities reflect current offer readiness, not proven profitability or measured market share.

## Evidence and limitations

The research supplied for this synthesis reports:

- Broad Kenyan hair-care and beauty/personal-care retail categories are substantial, but national product sales are not a measure of local loc-service revenue.
- Public listings show loc-focused providers in Eldoret, including Alif Locs, ADECARGO Dreadlocks, Flo Sisterlocks, Chimoto Dreadlocks and Randy Locs. Listings demonstrate competition, not market share or current customer volumes.
- SME digital-skills activity has been reported in Eldoret, and a national digital-hub needs assessment identified capability gaps. Neither proves willingness to pay for a specific WARENA GNU/Linux or systems-administration offer in Eldoret.
- Eldoret has recording, production and events infrastructure. This indicates an ecosystem, not automatic demand for a specific artist or performance.

**Verification requirement:** Before using numerical market claims or competitor prices in public-facing copy, attach and verify the original source URL, publication date, geographic scope, methodology and the exact claim supported. Public listings and advertised prices can change. This document does not treat the figures above as independently re-verified primary research.

The main evidence gap is customer-level behaviour: prices actually paid, booking frequency, unmet needs, service margins, repeat behaviour and willingness to pay.

## Geographic market

- **Primary:** Eldoret Town and customers that can be served conveniently.
- **Secondary:** surrounding Uasin Gishu communities where travel or remote delivery is practical.
- **Extended:** Kenya-wide and online customers for suitable digital and creative work.

The county population is not the addressable market. Service radius, travel, appointment capacity, delivery costs and customer-acquisition effort determine practical reach.

## CROWN — loc services

### Priority segments

**Existing loc wearers requiring maintenance:** likely to value neat roots, consistent results, convenience, transparent prices and confidence in the practitioner. Measure rebooking interval and repeat rate.

**Customers considering artificial locs:** likely to compare appearance, length, materials, time, maintenance and total cost. Confirm what the installation price includes before accepting a booking.

**Customers seeking dependable personal care:** test whether clear booking, respectful treatment, predictable pricing and schedule reliability affect decisions. Do not assume demographics alone predict willingness to pay.

### Current advertised starting prices

- Retouch & Styling — **from KES 700**
- Sisterlocs Retie — **from KES 1,500**
- Artificial Locs Installation — **from KES 3,500**

Keep these working starting prices during initial validation. Before advertising any service as fixed-price, document inclusions/exclusions, loc length and density adjustments, materials, expected duration, washing and aftercare, deposits, cancellations and any travel terms. No braiding is included in the current advertised scope.

Do not raise prices simply because a competitor advertises more. Calculate labour time, materials, overhead and rework, then compare conversion and customer feedback.

### Unmet needs to test — hypotheses, not established facts

- Customers want to know likely prices before starting a conversation.
- Customers need help identifying the right service for their loc condition.
- Customers want dependable appointment times and clear aftercare.
- Some customers may value home calls but reject the additional travel cost.

**First experiment:** publish a clear service menu and direct WhatsApp booking route. Track enquiry source, requested service, quote, confirmed booking, completed appointment, direct costs, time spent and repeat booking.

## DIGITAL / WARENA — technical services

### Candidate customer segments

1. Individuals and small teams with GNU/Linux installation, configuration, troubleshooting, backup or development-environment problems.
2. Small organisations that rely on computers and networks and experience disruptive systems problems.
3. Learners and practitioners seeking practical Linux or computing coaching.

These are candidate segments, not confirmed paying markets.

### Proposed pilot offers

These packages should be treated as proposals until their scope and delivery process are confirmed:

| Offer | Deliverable | Pricing approach |
|---|---|---|
| Linux Setup & Recovery | Agreed configuration or troubleshooting task with handover notes | Quote after a brief diagnostic |
| Technical Troubleshooting | Time-boxed session to investigate a defined issue | Publish a fee after duration and limits are known |
| Small-System Setup | Installation and configuration of one specified system/service | Fixed scope with exclusions |
| Backup & Recovery Readiness Review | Review of current arrangements and written recommendations | Fixed-price assessment |
| Linux / Computing Coaching | Practical session on a learner's specific task | Per session or workshop |
| Ongoing Systems Support | Scheduled support for defined systems, hours and response expectations | Monthly agreement only after pilot validation |

### Positioning

Lead with the problem and deliverable, not a list of technologies:

> Need help setting up or troubleshooting a Linux system? Describe what is not working. We will agree on the scope, explain the next steps and quote the work before proceeding.

Do not promise 24/7 support, certified cybersecurity, enterprise uptime, managed infrastructure or guaranteed recovery without the resources and evidence to deliver those commitments.

No reliable like-for-like local benchmark for specialist GNU/Linux administration has been established in this synthesis. Record actual hours, travel, materials, rework and customer feedback before setting a rate card.

## @donfelohke — creative work and revenue

Recording studios and event infrastructure are adjacent providers, but an artist's offer differs from a studio's production service. Potential audiences and buyers include:

- Genge and Kenyan music listeners.
- Event organisers and venues.
- Artists and producers seeking collaboration.
- Cultural and educational organisations.
- Digital-art, experimental-music and creative-technology audiences.

### Offer ladder

1. **Build evidence:** publish selected tracks, recordings, live-code demonstrations, movement studies and cultural commentary.
2. **Test paid demand:** offer a specific performance or collaboration with duration, technical requirements, preparation, travel and payment terms.
3. **Productise proven demand:** consider workshops, commissioned sound, digital works, releases or merchandise only after meaningful audience or buyer signals.

Follower counts and likes alone do not prove a paying market. Price work against preparation, rehearsal, equipment, transport, rights, venue requirements and delivery time.

## Comparative priorities

| Criterion | CROWN | DIGITAL / WARENA | Creative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer readiness | Higher: services and starting prices exist | Medium-low: needs a narrow validated offer | Depends on documented released work |
| Repeat potential | Worth measuring through rebooking | Possible if support needs recur | Variable by audience and booking type |
| Pricing clarity | Partial; scope must be explicit | Low; quote by defined scope | Offer-dependent |
| Key evidence gap | Conversion, retention and margins | Real buyer problems and willingness to pay | Audience-to-booking conversion |
| Next move | Improve booking and retention | Interview buyers; pilot scoped work | Publish work; test paid opportunities |

This is a sequencing recommendation, not a prediction of profit.

## 30-day validation plan

### Days 1–7 — baseline

- CROWN: log every enquiry, service request, quote, booking, completed job and follow-up.
- DIGITAL: interview at least five potential buyers about their last real technical problem, how they resolved it and what it cost.
- Creative: inventory published work, meaningful engagement, collaboration requests and paid opportunities.

**Deliverable:** a baseline register that separates observed activity from estimates.

### Days 8–14 — interviews

- CROWN: speak with 8–12 loc wearers, including existing and prospective customers.
- DIGITAL: speak with another 5–8 business owners, practitioners or learners.
- Creative: speak with 5–8 listeners, organisers, artists or cultural organisations.

Ask about actual past behaviour rather than hypothetical interest.

### Days 15–21 — real offer tests

- Present the CROWN menu with current starting prices and clearly stated scope.
- Offer one or two bounded DIGITAL pilot services at an agreed quote.
- Invite enquiries for a specific creative performance, workshop or collaboration deliverable.

Track qualified enquiries, bookings, deposits where appropriate, completed work and objections. Treat likes and verbal encouragement as secondary evidence.

### Days 22–30 — review

Compare enquiry-to-booking conversion, completed work, revenue, direct costs, time, cancellations, repeat interest and feedback. Retain offers that attract qualified demand and can be delivered sustainably; revise or pause offers that attract attention but do not convert.

## Interview prompts

### CROWN

- When was your last loc-maintenance appointment?
- What did you pay, and what did it include?
- What frustrates you about booking or maintenance?
- What would make you return to the same loctician?
- How do you balance price with confidence in the result?

### DIGITAL / WARENA

- Tell me about the last computer or systems problem that disrupted your work.
- What did you do, who helped, and what did it cost?
- What happened while it remained unresolved?
- Would you prefer a one-off fix, review, training or ongoing support?
- What evidence would you need before trusting an independent provider?

### @donfelohke

- Which work have you actually listened to, watched or attended?
- What made you return or share it?
- Have you paid for a similar performance, release or workshop?
- What would you consider booking this artist for?
- What would make the experience worth paying for?

Do not lead respondents toward agreement. Reasons not to buy are valuable findings.

## Product-development gate

Do not graduate a product from WIP until these are clear:

1. A defined buyer with a recognisable need.
2. A deliverable the buyer can understand and evaluate.
3. Evidence of demand, such as a paid pilot, deposit or repeated specific requests.
4. A viable delivery model with known time, cost, distribution and support.
5. A credible reason to choose NAKEBUSEN beyond novelty.

CROWN: validate recurring service demand first. DIGITAL: identify repeatable technical jobs. Creative: test releases, bookings, workshops or artefacts before producing inventory or financing events.

## Research limitations and source verification

This is a strategic synthesis and fieldwork plan, not a statistically representative survey. It does not establish NAKEBUSEN's market share, customer satisfaction, revenue potential, willingness-to-pay distribution or profitability.

Before external publication of market statistics, competitor ratings, review counts or advertised prices, add direct source links and verify that the pages remain current. Record source date, scope, methodology and the precise claim supported. Broad national market categories, samples from selected innovation hubs and online business listings must not be presented as representative measures of Eldoret demand.
